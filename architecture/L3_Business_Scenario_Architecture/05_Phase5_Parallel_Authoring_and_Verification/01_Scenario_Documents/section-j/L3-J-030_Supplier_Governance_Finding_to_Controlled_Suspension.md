# 2. Scenario Purpose

This scenario governs the controlled suspension of a supplier when an authorised governance finding shows that continued general supplier use presents risk that cannot be contained adequately through a narrower restriction. It provides urgent protection, due process, explicit authority, prospective control, review, remediation, and reinstatement or escalation paths.

Suspension is not deletion, blacklisting, debarment, permanent exclusion, contract termination, or financial reversal. Those consequences require their own governed authority and evidence.

---

# 3. Business Outcome

A material supplier governance finding reaches Controlled Suspension with defined grounds, supplier and related scope, effective time, affected future activity, existing-position referrals, supplier notice, response and review rights, remediation, duration, periodic review, reinstatement or escalation criteria, approval, and immutable audit history.

Outcome labels in this section are business dispositions or supporting-record results, not additional canonical lifecycle states. Section 13 states the only canonical lifecycle impact.

---

# 4. Scenario Boundary and Scope

## 4.1 Start Boundary

The scenario begins when a governed supplier finding, restriction breach, repeated material incident, mandatory compliance consequence, or urgent institutional risk is referred to the authorised suspension authority.

## 4.2 End Boundary

The scenario ends when suspension is rejected, returned, approved, varied, continued, lifted, or referred to a separate terminal-disposition process; authorised consumers receive the current status; affected open positions are referred to their owners; and review, remediation, appeal, reinstatement, or escalation paths are recorded.

## 4.3 In Scope

- validating the governance finding, supplier identity, evidence lineage, seriousness, urgency, recurrence, scope, and current exposure;
- determining whether narrower restrictions are inadequate, breached, unavailable, or legally insufficient;
- imposing urgent interim or approved controlled suspension under defined authority;
- suspending new invitations, participation, evaluation advancement, award approval, new commitment, or other approved future use across the defined supplier scope;
- identifying pending sourcing, awards, proposed commitments, executed commitments, deliveries, warranties, disputes, payables, and payments for separate authoritative review;
- governing supplier notice, response, hearing or review, remediation, periodic reassessment, continuation, variation, reinstatement, and escalation;
- preserving the distinction between suspension and blacklisting, debarment, permanent exclusion, closure, or contract termination.

## 4.4 Out of Scope

- creating the underlying assessment or risk finding;
- imposing blacklisting, debarment, permanent exclusion, or another terminal supplier disposition;
- automatically cancelling, terminating, rescinding, or amending a Purchase Order or contract;
- reversing receipts, inventory, payables, accounting entries, payments, recoveries, or bank transactions;
- deciding criminal, civil, disciplinary, tax, sanctions, or regulatory liability outside the relevant authority;
- technical enforcement, case management, notification, or integration implementation.

# 7. Preconditions and Entry Criteria

- The supplier and any affected branch, site, consortium member, or controlled related entity can be linked canonically.
- A governed finding or urgent authorised basis exists with source, facts, severity, scope, evidence lineage, and responsible owner.
- Suspension grounds, authority, urgent duration, notice, response, hearing or review, confidentiality, conflicts, monitoring, remediation, continuation, lifting, reinstatement, and escalation rules are effective.
- Existing restrictions and their effectiveness or breaches are known.
- Current supplier approval, qualifications, sourcing, awards, commitments, contracts, and relevant financial positions can be identified.
- Sensitive evidence and legally restricted disclosures can be protected without eliminating required decision evidence.

---

# 8. Trigger

An authorised supplier governance finding indicates material, systemic, repeated, urgent, mandatory, or uncontrollable risk for which ordinary monitoring or scoped restriction is insufficient.

---

# 9. Main Business Flow

1. The Institutional Verifier submits the canonical supplier, finding, facts, source, severity, urgency, affected scope, prior restrictions, breaches, current exposure, and requested action.
2. The Supplier Governance Authority validates authority, evidence lineage, identity, related entities, current supplier state, duplicate cases, active restrictions, disputes, investigations, and mandatory rules.
3. Where delay creates unacceptable legal, safety, security, fraud, sanctions, data, public-interest, financial, or continuity risk, an urgent action authority may impose a defined interim suspension.
4. Interim suspension records reasons, exact scope, effective time, maximum duration, permitted emergency activity, compensating controls, and mandatory final-review deadline.
5. The institution identifies supplier approval, qualifications, categories, sites, sourcing events, evaluations, recommendations, awards, proposed commitments, executed commitments, deliveries, warranties, claims, disputes, payables, and pending payments.
6. Responsibility and scope are distinguished across supplier, site, branch, consortium member, subcontractor, predecessor, successor, parent, subsidiary, and other related entities; suspension is not inherited automatically.
7. Conflicts involving Institutional Verifier, investigators, sourcing or contract teams, supplier, approvers, or related parties are declared and mitigated.
8. The case evaluates whether narrower restriction can contain risk and records why suspension is necessary, proportionate, and consistent with comparable cases.
9. The supplier receives sufficient particulars, proposed consequence, response period, evidence channel, review rights, and remediation expectations where lawful and compatible with urgent, privileged, confidential, or investigative constraints.
10. Supplier response, contrary evidence, cooperation, corrective action, root cause, recurrence, prior history, mitigating and aggravating factors, and residual risk are assessed.
11. Specialists review mandatory and cross-domain consequences, while existing transaction and financial owners retain their own authority.
12. The recommendation defines grounds, supplier and related scope, affected and permitted activity, effective time, duration, periodic review, remediation, reinstatement, exceptions, and escalation conditions.
13. The Supplier Governance Authority reviews evidence sufficiency, jurisdiction, urgency, conflicts, due process, consistency, proportionality, supplier response, continuity risk, and proposed safeguards.
14. The authority rejects, returns, approves, varies, continues, lifts, or refers the case for separate terminal disposition.
15. An approved suspension becomes an immutable authoritative supplier governance status; prior Active, qualification, restriction, and assessment records remain historical facts.
16. Authorised sourcing, qualification, award, commitment, contract, finance, legal, safety, security, operational, and audit consumers receive the minimum suspension facts required to review their positions.
17. Notices, responses, reviews, remediation, exceptions, breaches, continuation, variation, lifting, reinstatement, escalation, actors, timestamps, and versions remain auditable.

# 10. Alternate Business Flows

## 10.1 Urgent Interim Suspension

Immediate protection is applied within urgent authority. It expires, converts, varies, or lifts only through the mandated timely review; it cannot remain indefinitely without decision.

## 10.2 Proposed Suspension Rejected

The authority may record no action, monitoring, or scoped restriction under L3-J-029. The finding and rejection reasons remain visible.

## 10.3 Partial Supplier Scope

Only the legally and evidentially attributable branch, site, category, consortium member, or controlled scope is suspended. Unaffected scope remains governed by its current status.

## 10.4 Supplier Remediation During Suspension

Evidence is received and independently verified. Suspension continues until the authorised reinstatement decision becomes effective.

## 10.5 Existing Contract or Critical Service

The contract and operational owners decide continuity, controlled performance, substitution, termination, or other action under their authority. Any permitted continuation requires explicit grounds and controls and does not restore general supplier eligibility.

## 10.6 Active Investigation

Disclosure may be limited lawfully, but the authority records sufficient protected grounds. Review timing coordinates with the investigation without allowing uncontrolled use.

## 10.7 Escalation to Terminal Disposition

Where evidence may justify blacklisting, debarment, permanent exclusion, or closure, the case is referred separately. Suspension may remain interim protection and does not predetermine the final outcome.

# 11. Failure, Interruption and Business Recovery

## 11.1 Supplier Identity or Related-Entity Scope Ambiguous

The case is held or narrowly controlled under urgent authority until canonical linkage and attribution are resolved.

## 11.2 Evidence Insufficient for Final Suspension

The proposal is returned, rejected, narrowed to restriction, or maintained only as an authorised interim control within its deadline. Allegation is not recorded as final fact.

## 11.3 Suspension Fails to Propagate

The failure is recorded, publication is reissued, affected activity is reconciled, and unauthorised actions are referred to their owners. Authoritative effective time remains visible.

## 11.4 Unauthorised Use or Override

The attempt is blocked or escalated. Any resulting sourcing, award, commitment, receipt, payable, or payment position is preserved and reviewed by its authoritative owner.

## 11.5 Due-Process Deadline Missed

The case is escalated. Interim action follows approved expiry or extension rules and cannot become permanent by administrative delay.

## 11.6 Incorrect Suspension

The authority issues an attributable correction or lifting decision and coordinates impact review. Original decision, affected activity, harm, and corrective action remain auditable.

## 11.7 System Interruption

Business-effective suspension remains controlling. On recovery, status and affected activity are reconciled without silent deletion or backdating.

## 11.8 Reinstatement Decision Not Propagated

Suspension remains operationally controlling until the authoritative effective reinstatement is reliably published; failed publication is reconciled promptly without altering decision history.

# 12. Business Rules and Decision Constraints

- Suspension requires an authorised governance finding or express urgent basis, canonical linkage, reasons, scope, authority, effective time, and review conditions.
- Suspension is used when risk cannot be contained adequately through monitoring or governed restriction.
- Urgent interim suspension is narrow, time-bound, reviewed promptly, and cannot become indefinite through inaction.
- Supplier response and review rights apply unless lawful urgent, confidential, privileged, or investigative limitations exist.
- Related entities are not suspended automatically without evidence, control relationship, and applicable authority.
- Suspension controls future supplier use prospectively; it does not delete historical approval, qualification, performance, sourcing, or contract records.
- Suspension does not automatically cancel an award, Purchase Order, contract, receipt, payable, accounting entry, payment, warranty, claim, or recovery.
- Any controlled continuation under an existing commitment requires separate authority, reasons, scope, time, and safeguards and does not restore general eligibility.
- Remediation submission does not equal reinstatement; independent verification and authorised decision are required.
- Suspension is not blacklisting, debarment, permanent exclusion, closure, or terminal disposition.
- Each continuation, variation, exception, lifting, reinstatement, and escalation creates an attributable prospective decision; original history remains immutable.

# 15. Business Authorization, Approval and Audit Obligations

## 15.1 Authorization and Separation

The canonical J02 roles are separated as policy requires: Supplier Representative; Institutional Verifier; and Supplier Governance Authority. The Supplier Representative cannot verify, approve, or remove an institutional restriction on itself, and a conflicted verifier cannot decide the governed outcome.

## 15.2 Required Evidence

- canonical supplier and related entities, governance finding, evidence references, severity, urgency, recurrence, prior restrictions, breaches, current exposure, and affected scope;
- supplier approval, qualification, sourcing, award, commitment, contract, operational, financial, remediation, investigation, and related status positions;
- conflicts, specialist findings, supplier notice and response, due-process limitations, alternatives, proportionality, continuity assessment, residual risk, and recommendation;
- urgent action, final approval, reasons, effective time, duration, reviews, exceptions, controlled continuation, remediation, continuation, variation, lifting, reinstatement or escalation, actors, timestamps, and versions.

## 15.3 Audit Obligations

The institution must reconstruct why suspension was considered, why restriction was insufficient, which evidence and authority supported interim and final action, what due process occurred, which supplier scope and downstream positions were affected, who approved exceptions or continuation, whether remediation was verified, and why suspension was lifted, continued, or escalated.

# 16. Completion Criteria and Postconditions

## 16.1 Completion Criteria

The scenario is complete only when all of the following criteria are satisfied:

- a governance finding has been substantiated by authoritative evidence and reviewed under institutional governance;
- a suspension decision has been made by an authority appropriate to the finding severity, exposure, and institutional risk;
- the suspension scope, effective date, duration, conditions, review timeline, and appeal mechanism are explicit and attributable;
- existing commitments, active contracts, open purchase orders, and in-progress sourcing events are assessed and governed under their authoritative owners;
- the suspension decision, evidence, authority, actors, conditions, appeals outcome, and audit trail are preserved and immutable;
- the supplier is unable to participate in new sourcing events, receive new awards, or enter new commitments during the suspension period.


## 16.2 Postconditions

Upon successful scenario completion, the following institutional postconditions hold:

- the supplier is in a Suspended state for the defined scope and duration with an explicit effective date;
- all new sourcing activity for the suspended supplier is blocked from the effective time of the suspension;
- existing obligations are governed by their authoritative domain owners under the disposition established at suspension time;
- the supplier's suspension decision, evidence, authority, conditions, and appeal rights are permanently preserved and auditable;
- reinstatement requires a governed remediation outcome under L3-J-032 or a separate authoritative governance decision with explicit conditions.

- Existing contractual and financial consequences remain with their authoritative scenarios.
