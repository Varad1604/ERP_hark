# 2. Scenario Purpose

This scenario governs the exceptional conversion of a severe, substantiated supplier governance finding into an authorised Blacklisted Status. It ensures that terminal or long-duration exclusion is imposed only through lawful authority, reliable evidence, due process, proportional scope, defined duration or permanence, and a fully reconstructable decision.

Blacklisting is distinct from assessment, restriction, suspension, contract termination, financial withholding, supplier closure, and archival. It cannot arise automatically from a score, allegation, expired qualification, inactive relationship, or ordinary performance issue.

---

# 3. Business Outcome

A severe supplier governance finding reaches an approved, rejected, returned, or otherwise governed blacklisting decision with defined supplier scope, grounds, jurisdiction, effective period, prohibited activity, related-entity treatment, supplier notice, representation and appeal rights, publication limits, downstream referrals, authority, and immutable evidence.

Outcome labels in this section are business dispositions or supporting-record results, not additional canonical lifecycle states. Section 13 states the only canonical lifecycle impact.

---

# 4. Scenario Boundary and Scope

## 4.1 Start Boundary

The scenario begins when an authorised governance, legal, compliance, vigilance, audit, fraud, safety, security, ethical, regulatory, or suspension authority refers a severe substantiated finding for blacklisting consideration.

## 4.2 End Boundary

The scenario ends when the Supplier Governance Authority rejects, returns, approves, varies, stays, or withdraws the proposed blacklisting; the effective status and permitted disclosure are published; affected positions are referred to their authoritative owners; and appeal, review, expiry, or restoration eligibility is recorded.

## 4.3 In Scope

- validating identity, jurisdiction, authority, evidence lineage, severity, intent, recurrence, materiality, and finality of findings;
- confirming that restriction or suspension is insufficient where required;
- issuing notice, particulars, representation opportunity, hearing or review, and reasoned determination;
- defining supplier, branch, site, consortium, category, geographic, institutional, programme, funding, and related-entity scope;
- defining fixed-term, indefinite, permanent, or other legally permitted duration and effective time;
- prohibiting new registration reliance, qualification, invitation, participation, evaluation, award, or commitment within approved scope;
- publishing minimum necessary status to authorised consumers and governing appeal, stay, expiry, review, correction, and restoration referral.

## 4.4 Out of Scope

- investigating or creating the original finding;
- automatically terminating contracts or cancelling Purchase Orders;
- reversing receipts, inventory, payables, accounting entries, payments, recoveries, or bank transactions;
- imposing criminal, civil, employment, tax, or regulatory liability outside competent authority;
- deleting the supplier identity or historical records;
- treating inactivity, expiry, ordinary nonperformance, or relationship closure as blacklisting;
- technical enforcement, publication, integration, or case-management implementation.

# 7. Preconditions and Entry Criteria

- The supplier and any proposed related scope are canonically identifiable.
- A severe governed finding exists with reliable evidence, authority, reasons, and current relevance.
- The competent blacklisting, notice, representation, hearing, appeal, stay, duration, disclosure, correction, expiry, and restoration rules are effective.
- Interim protective action is governed separately under L3-J-030 where required.
- Conflicts and separation of investigation, recommendation, decision, and appeal can be controlled.
- Sensitive, privileged, whistleblower, personal, security, and investigative evidence can be protected lawfully.

# 8. Trigger

A competent authority receives a severe supplier governance finding that may justify terminal, permanent, indefinite, or fixed-term exclusion from future institutional procurement activity.

# 9. Main Business Flow

1. The Supplier Governance Authority records the canonical supplier, finding, evidence lineage, legal or policy grounds, severity, intent, recurrence, impact, prior actions, current suspension, and proposed scope under applicable separation rules.
2. The Institutional Verifier validates referral authority, identity, jurisdiction, evidentiary status, related proceedings, applicable rules, limitation periods, and current protective controls.
3. The institution separates allegation, interim finding, substantiated fact, conviction or regulatory order, contractual dispute, and final governance finding.
4. Any related-entity, branch, proprietor, partner, director, consortium, successor, or affiliate scope is supported by evidence and applicable control or attribution rules, not assumed.
5. Conflicts involving investigators, complainants, sourcing teams, contract owners, decision-makers, or related parties are declared and mitigated.
6. Legal and specialist reviewers confirm authority, grounds, due-process requirements, disclosure limits, mandatory consequences, and whether parallel proceedings affect timing.
7. The supplier receives notice containing sufficient particulars, proposed scope and duration, response method, evidence access permitted by law, deadline, hearing or review route, and consequence of non-response.
8. The supplier's representation, contrary evidence, mitigation, cooperation, remediation, prior history, recurrence, intent, benefit, harm, and public-interest factors are assessed.
9. The Institutional Verifier evaluates no blacklisting, continued suspension, fixed-term exclusion, indefinite or permanent exclusion where lawful, and narrower scope alternatives.
10. The recommendation records proven grounds, rejected allegations, affected and unaffected scope, duration, effective time, prohibited activity, review or expiry conditions, and publication limits.
11. The Supplier Governance Authority reviews evidence sufficiency, jurisdiction, fairness, consistency, proportionality, conflicts, supplier representation, legal advice, and institutional interest under applicable separation rules.
12. The authority issues a reasoned decision to reject, return, approve, narrow, vary, or otherwise dispose of the proposal.
13. Approved status becomes effective only according to the decision and any applicable stay or appeal rule; it is never silently backdated.
14. The authoritative Blacklisted Status is linked immutably to the decision, scope, duration, grounds classification, authority, notice, appeal path, and disclosure classification.
15. Authorised registration, qualification, sourcing, award, commitment, contract, finance, legal, compliance, and audit owners receive the minimum necessary status for their decisions.
16. Existing awards, contracts, deliveries, payables, payments, claims, warranties, and recoveries are referred to their owners and are not rewritten automatically.
17. Representations, decisions, appeals, stays, reviews, corrections, expiry, restoration eligibility, publications, actors, timestamps, and versions remain auditable.

# 10. Alternate Business Flows

## 10.1 Proposal Rejected or Returned

The authority records reasons and may retain suspension, narrow restriction, monitoring, or no action. No Blacklisted Status is created.

## 10.2 Fixed-Term Blacklisting

The decision defines start, end, review requirements, continuing conditions, and whether eligibility restoration requires L3-J-032 after expiry.

## 10.3 Indefinite or Permanent Blacklisting

Such status is permitted only where lawful and expressly authorised, with stated review or non-review conditions and no implied automatic restoration.

## 10.4 Appeal or Judicial / Administrative Stay

The stay, scope, effective time, and permitted activity are recorded without deleting the original decision. Consumers receive the current controlling position.

## 10.5 Supplier Does Not Respond

The authority may proceed after valid service and response period where allowed. Non-response is not treated automatically as admission.

## 10.6 Related Entity Proposed

Each additional entity receives evidence-based attribution and required due process. Common ownership alone does not automatically extend blacklisting.

## 10.7 Existing Critical Contract

The contract and operational owners decide continuation, safeguards, substitution, or termination separately. Controlled continuation does not restore future eligibility.

# 11. Failure, Interruption and Business Recovery

## 11.1 Defective Notice or Authority

Final decision is held, corrected, or remanded. Interim suspension continues only under valid separate authority.

## 11.2 Evidence Lost, Altered, or Legally Unusable

The limitation is recorded and the case is returned, narrowed, or rejected unless sufficient lawful evidence remains.

## 11.3 Status Publication Failure

Publication is reissued and affected actions are reconciled. Decision-effective time and notification failure remain distinct facts.

## 11.4 Incorrect Entity or Overbroad Scope

The authority issues a correction, variation, stay, or withdrawal. Original status and impact remain visible.

## 11.5 Unauthorised Override

The attempt is blocked and escalated. Any resulting procurement or financial action is preserved for review.

## 11.6 Appeal Outcome Not Propagated

The controlling outcome is reissued, current eligibility is reconciled, and inconsistent decisions are referred to their owners.

## 11.7 System Interruption

The legal business-effective decision remains controlling. Recovery reconciles status without deletion or backdating.

# 12. Business Rules and Decision Constraints

- Blacklisting requires severe substantiated grounds, competent authority, lawful procedure, reasoned decision, explicit scope, and effective period.
- Assessment, restriction, suspension, qualification expiry, inactivity, closure, or a system score cannot automatically create Blacklisted Status.
- Notice, representation, hearing or review, and appeal rights follow applicable law and policy.
- Interim suspension protects pending decision but does not predetermine blacklisting.
- Related entities are included only through evidence, applicable attribution rules, authority, and required due process.
- Blacklisting governs future eligibility and does not delete historical supplier identity or evidence.
- It does not automatically terminate contracts or reverse receipts, payables, accounting entries, or payments.
- Status disclosure is limited to lawful, accurate, current, and necessary information.
- Appeal, stay, variation, expiry, correction, and restoration create attributable successor facts and preserve original history.
- Restoration, where permitted, requires a separate governed decision under L3-J-032.

# 15. Business Authorization, Approval and Audit Obligations

## 15.1 Authorization and Separation

The canonical J02 roles are separated as policy requires: Supplier Representative; Institutional Verifier; and Supplier Governance Authority. The Supplier Representative cannot verify, approve, or remove an institutional restriction on itself, and a conflicted verifier cannot decide the governed outcome.

## 15.2 Required Evidence

- canonical identity and proposed related scope, findings, source evidence, prior restrictions and suspension, legal and policy grounds, severity, recurrence, intent, impact, jurisdiction, and authority;
- notice, delivery evidence, disclosed particulars, representation, hearing or review record, conflicts, specialist advice, alternatives, proportionality, and recommendation;
- reasoned decision, scope, duration, effective time, prohibited activity, publication classification, appeals, stays, variations, corrections, expiry, actors, timestamps, and versions.

## 15.3 Audit Obligations

The institution must reconstruct the proven grounds, evidence and authority, notice and representation, consideration of alternatives, decision reasoning, scope and duration, publications, downstream reliance, appeals and stays, and every later change to the status.

# 16. Completion Criteria and Postconditions

## 16.1 Completion Criteria

The scenario is complete only when all of the following criteria are satisfied:

- a severe governance finding has been substantiated by authoritative evidence meeting the institutional threshold for blacklisting;
- a blacklisting decision has been made by an authority with the delegated power to impose this terminal status;
- the blacklisting scope, effective date, grounds, legal basis, appeal mechanism, and institutional consequences are explicit and attributable;
- existing commitments, active contracts, outstanding awards, and in-progress sourcing events are assessed and governed under their authoritative owners;
- the blacklisting decision, evidence, authority, actors, grounds, and audit trail are preserved and immutable;
- the supplier is permanently prevented from participating in institutional sourcing, receiving new awards, or entering new commitments for the defined scope.


## 16.2 Postconditions

Upon successful scenario completion, the following institutional postconditions hold:

- the supplier is in a Blacklisted state with an explicit effective date, grounds, and blacklisting authority permanently on record;
- all new sourcing activity for the blacklisted supplier is irreversibly blocked unless a governed appeal or reversal is separately authorised;
- existing obligations are governed by their authoritative domain owners under the disposition established at blacklisting time;
- the blacklisting decision, evidence, authority, appeal process, and outcome are permanently preserved and auditable;
- reinstatement requires an explicit authoritative governance reversal decision with documented grounds—not a routine remediation pathway.
